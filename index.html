MX-5
Buyer's guide · Mazda MX-5

Rust is the enemy, not the engine

The MX-5 is famously reliable mechanically; the things to check are underneath. Look hard at the sills and rear arches, make sure the hood drain holes are clear so water isn't pooling in the cabin, and check the hood itself for tears. Later cars also came as a folding hard-top (RHT) if you want a coupé for winter.

Boxster
Buyer's guide · Porsche Boxster

Buy the history, then the car

Cheap Boxsters are the older 986 and 987 generations. The well-known worries on these flat-sixes are the intermediate shaft (IMS) bearing and bore scoring, so a specialist inspection and a thick service file are worth more than low miles. Check for oil leaks at the rear main seal, the coolant tank and the condition of the hood's rear window.

Z4
Buyer's guide · BMW Z4

Straight-six soundtrack on a budget

The first-generation Z4 (E85) has a fabric roof; the second (E89, from 2009) has a folding metal hard-top. Test the roof through several cycles, as roof hydraulics and motors can fail. Many came on run-flat tyres, which some owners swap for a smoother ride. Six-cylinder cars are the ones to have for the noise.

SLK
Buyer's guide · Mercedes SLK

The all-seasons convertible

The SLK's Vario-roof turns it from coupé to roadster at the push of a button, so watch it work fully, more than once, before buying. Check for rust around the boot lid and wheel arches on older cars and look for a stamped service book. It is a cruiser more than a back-road weapon, and best judged that way.

TT
Buyer's guide · Audi TT Roadster

Design-classic style, daily usability

The second-generation (8J) TT Roadster has an electric fabric roof and turbocharged petrol engines. Ask about oil consumption on the 2.0 TFSI, check DSG/S tronic gearboxes shift cleanly, and make sure the roof and its electrics work. Quattro versions add all-weather grip.

Roadster
Buyer's guide · Toyota MR2 Roadster

Featherweight mid-engined fun

The MR2 Roadster (1999–2007) puts its engine behind you and weighs very little, so it feels quick without being fast. Early engines could burn oil, so ask about it and check the oil level. Storage is tiny, and the front boot is home to the spare wheel. Check the rear subframe for corrosion.
